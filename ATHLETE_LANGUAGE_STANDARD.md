# Merrill Girls Wrestling Athlete Language Standard

Athlete-facing language is part of the performance system.

## Performance cue rule

When the athlete needs to perform, tell her what to do rather than what to avoid.

Examples:

- "Win first contact."
- "Easy breath. Find your stance."
- "Settle. Stance. First job."
- "Next exchange."
- "Clean setup. Finish."

This is not a blanket ban on words such as "no" or "never." Safety and privacy language must remain clear. The rule applies especially to performance instructions, corrective cues, competition preparation, reset routines, and post-mistake responses.

## Athlete language should

- direct attention toward controllable actions;
- use concrete wrestling language;
- reduce cognitive load under pressure;
- make mistakes useful information;
- reinforce mastery, learning, competence, and preparation;
- support athlete choice when choice is useful;
- build confidence from evidence;
- keep recovery affirmative;
- use short instructional cues during performance;
- sound like a good wrestling coach speaking to one athlete.

## Athlete language should avoid

- clinical disclaimers in normal athlete workflows;
- engineering or Firebase terminology;
- shame and social-comparison framing;
- appearance-centered or weight-centered motivation;
- generic hype when a useful action cue is available;
- avoidance instructions when an affirmative target cue can communicate the desired action;
- childish, fragile, or patronizing wording.

## Merrill program language

Preserve:

- Today's 1%
- Went Well
- Improve
- Confidence Bank
- YOU University
- Five Cs: Commitment, Courage, Concentration, Control, Confidence
- Core Four: Persistent, Consistent, Resilient, Relentless

Pillar breathing remains:

- inhale: Persistent
- hold: Consistent
- exhale: Resilient
- hold: Relentless

The athlete should experience the performance science without needing to know the scientific vocabulary behind it.
